# Backfeed your house wiring with Home Assistant

The proven way to turn an old landline phone into a Home Assistant voice assistant is to plug it into a **Grandstream HT801 analog telephone adapter (ATA)** set to **"Offhook Auto-Dial"** straight to Home Assistant's built-in **VoIP integration**. Lifting the handset opens an Assist conversation, and hanging up ends it. Home Assistant published this recipe in April 2023 as "the world's most private voice assistant", and nearly every later guide copies it ([HA blog](https://home-assistant.io/blog/2023/04/27/year-of-the-voice-chapter-2); [HA tutorial](https://www.home-assistant.io/voice_control/worlds-most-private-voice-assistant/)). Your situation suits it unusually well. The HT801's single FXS port supplies everything a real phone line does: battery, dial tone, ringing and hook detection, rated at **5 REN**. Once the dead telco drop is disconnected at the NID, you can "backfeed" the port into any basement jack and every wall jack in the house goes live ([WebArray](https://webarray.tawk.help/article/how-to-use-one-ata-for-an-entire-house); [Grandstream FAQ](https://documentation.grandstream.com/knowledge-base/ht8xx-v1-faq/)). For a fully local pipeline, use Speech-to-Phrase for speech-to-text (with optional faster-whisper fallback), Piper for text-to-speech, and Home Assistant's built-in intent agent, on an x86 mini PC rather than a Raspberry Pi 4. One builder measured about **1 s per command on an N100 versus 8–9 s on a Pi 4** ([SmartHomeScene](https://smarthomescene.com/guides/voice-calling-home-assistant-with-an-old-phone)). Plan expansion around one fact: **HA's VoIP integration uses only one port per ATA and identifies phones by ATA**. Phones in parallel on one pair act as a single assistant. Each independent phone needs its own ATA and its own wire pair, or you need a PBX layer in between. The one-phone build costs roughly $45–55 in telephony hardware on top of whatever runs Home Assistant.

## Four build families exist, and only two can use house wiring

The documented projects fall into four families, and what separates them is where the analog line ends. The **ATA family** includes the official HA tutorial, SmartHomeScene's three-phone fully local reproduction from December 2023, and FreePBX- or Asterisk-bridged variants. These builds present a genuine FXS line, so the unmodified phone neither knows nor cares that no telco is on the other end ([HA tutorial](https://www.home-assistant.io/voice_control/worlds-most-private-voice-assistant/); [SmartHomeScene](https://smarthomescene.com/guides/voice-calling-home-assistant-with-an-old-phone)). The **SLIC family** emulates the central office itself on an ESP32 with a Silvertel AG1171 line-interface chip. Examples are Analog Redial, written up in November 2026 (ESP32-C6, ES8388 codec, one or two AG1171s, SIP to Asterisk, HA integration only "experimental" via MQTT/webhooks), and weeBell ([ElectronicsForU](https://www.electronicsforu.com/?p=207039); [GitHub](https://github.com/sdylewski/Analog-Redial); [Hackaday.io](https://hackaday.io/project/191002-weebell-personal-central-office-for-pots-phones)). The **ESP-inside-the-phone family** guts the phone instead. The best example is Tobias Dyballa's ESPHome "Tabletop Phone" (March 2024), which replaces the microphone with an I2S mic, drives the original speaker with a 3 W I2S amp, and wires the hook switch and keys to GPIOs ([HA forum](https://community.home-assistant.io/t/tabletop-phone-voice-assistant/699589); [GitHub](https://github.com/t0bit0bsen/Voice-Assistant-FeTAp)). The newest family is the **in-HA PBX**: the HACS "VoIP Stack" project makes HA itself a SIP registrar with extensions, ring groups, DTMF-driven automations, and Assist as a dialable extension ([GitHub](https://github.com/n-IA-hane/esphome-intercom); [HA forum](https://community.home-assistant.io/t/voip-stack-for-home-assistant-and-esphome/1016178)).

ESP-inside builds bypass phone wiring entirely; they need Wi-Fi and power at the phone. That rules them out for your goal of a basement device receiving the signal over existing copper. The SLIC route can drive house wiring, but it rings weakly (about **60 Vrms into one phone, more than 40 Vrms into three**). It also has no native Assist path and needs a chip with a **16-week DigiKey lead time** ([Ag1171 datasheet](https://static.chipdip.ru/lib/300/DOC029300244.pdf); [DigiKey](https://www.digikey.com/en/products/detail/silvertel/AG1171/21187236)). The ATA route wins on every axis that matters here: it is officially supported, cheap, rated for 1 km of 24 AWG loop, and requires no soldering. One caveat: no documented project explicitly wires an HA-connected ATA into in-wall house wiring. That step rests on standard ATA whole-house backfeed practice, which is well documented for VoIP service, not on an HA-specific write-up.

## Recommended architecture: one HT801 at the demarc, one mini PC, zero cloud

In the recommended architecture, the HT801 sits in the basement next to the NID or a basement jack. A short RJ11 cord runs from its green FXS port into the house's line-1 pair, and its blue port connects by Ethernet to your LAN. Configured for Offhook Auto-Dial, the ATA places an IP-direct SIP call to HA the moment any phone on the pair goes off-hook. No PBX, no SIP registration and no dialing are involved ([HA tutorial](https://www.home-assistant.io/voice_control/worlds-most-private-voice-assistant/)). HA listens on **UDP 5060**, accepts **only Opus** (default payload type 123), decodes to 16 kHz mono PCM for speech-to-text, and auto-creates one Assist satellite device per calling SIP URI. Each device carries an "Allow calls" switch (off by default), a per-phone pipeline select, and a "finished speaking detection" sensitivity select ([voip-utils sip.py](https://github.com/home-assistant-libs/voip-utils/blob/master/voip_utils/sip.py); [select.py](https://github.com/home-assistant/core/blob/dev/homeassistant/components/voip/select.py); [switch.py](https://github.com/home-assistant/core/blob/dev/homeassistant/components/voip/switch.py)). Since 2025.2, the path also runs in reverse. **`assist_satellite.announce` rings the phone** (for up to a hard-coded 30 s) and plays TTS in a loop until hang-up, and `start_conversation` speaks once and then listens ([HA 2025.2](https://www.home-assistant.io/blog/2025/02/05/release-20252/); [assist_satellite.py](https://github.com/home-assistant/core/blob/dev/homeassistant/components/voip/assist_satellite.py)).

For the "fully local" requirement, the pipeline matters more than the phone. HA's local guide offers three engines. **Speech-to-Phrase** is a closed-vocabulary engine that transcribes in under 1 s on a Pi 4 and about 150 ms on a Pi 5. **Whisper** is open-ended and takes about 8 s on a Pi 4 but under 1 s on a NUC. **Piper** handles text-to-speech, generating 1.6 s of speech per second even on a Pi ([HA local guide](https://www.home-assistant.io/voice_control/voice_remote_local_assistant/); [Speech-to-Phrase blog](https://www.home-assistant.io/blog/2025/02/13/voice-chapter-9-speech-to-phrase/)). A phone line strengthens the case for Speech-to-Phrase. The Opus leg is wideband, but the analog handset and FXS hybrid still pass roughly telephone-band audio, and Whisper large-v3 degrades sharply on real phone recordings: **2.7% word error rate on clean speech versus 17.7% on call-center audio** in one vendor benchmark, with other benchmarks worse and inconsistent ([AssemblyAI](https://assemblyai.com/blog/comparing-speech-to-text-apis-on-phone-call-transcription); [Deepgram](https://deepgram.com/blog/whisper-v3-results)). A recognizer that only has to pick among your own entity names and sentences is far more robust on that signal.

The recommended host is therefore an **Intel N100-class mini PC running HA OS**. Run Speech-to-Phrase as the primary STT, with faster-whisper small-int8 as a fallback; community logs estimate the fallback at 1–3 s on an N100 CPU ([maloyan.xyz](https://maloyan.xyz/blog/home-assistant-local-voice-stack-2026); [Smart Home Field Guide](https://smarthomefieldguide.com/blog/local-voice-assistant-home-assistant-build-log/)). Add Piper and the built-in conversation agent. A local LLM is optional and belongs on a separate GPU box. In August 2026 measurements, Qwen2.5-14B reached **95.8% command accuracy at 371 ms warm** on an RTX 5080, while 3B models misfired badly enough to generate whole-house "off" commands. CPU-only LLM prefill takes "seconds, not milliseconds" ([TechFuel HQ](https://techfuelhq.com/tutorials/home-assistant-local-llm-voice-2026/)). Keep LLM replies short whatever you choose. The VoIP integration joins all TTS audio before sending it, so HA's 2025 streaming-TTS speedup, which cut time-to-first-speech from more than 5 s to about 0.5 s, **does not apply on a phone** ([assist_satellite.py](https://github.com/home-assistant/core/blob/dev/homeassistant/components/voip/assist_satellite.py); [Voice chapter 11](https://www.home-assistant.io/blog/2025/10/22/voice-chapter-11)).

## Isolate, backfeed, verify: the wiring and setup sequence

Wiring safety comes first because the voltages are real. An FXS port puts about **-48 VDC on the pair on-hook** and rings at up to about **90–100 VAC**, enough for a painful shock ([UBC ECE notes](https://people.ece.ubc.ca/edc/4550.fall2017/lec2.pdf); [Mike Holt forum](https://forums.mikeholt.com/threads/equipment-protection.26174/post-26174)). The single most important rule: the house pair must be **physically disconnected from the telco side at the NID** before the ATA is attached. Otherwise telco battery or ringing backfeeds into the adapter and can "fry" it, and the incoming red/green pair must never touch the backfed wiring ([Sundance forum](https://sundance-communications.com/forum/ubbthreads.php/topics/637277); [AT&T forum](https://forums.att.com/t5/Setup-Self-Install-TV/Self-Install-Backfeed-to-existing-phone-circuit/m-p/2644823)). Most NIDs have a customer-side test jack; unplugging its short modular plug isolates the inside wiring. Older protector blocks without one require cutting the incoming pair and terminating it ([homephonewiring.com](https://homephonewiring.com/nid.html); [WebArray](https://webarray.tawk.help/article/how-to-use-one-ata-for-an-entire-house)). Leave the telco-side protector and its ground intact. Lightning can induce surges above 2 kV on inside wiring, and the protector is what clamps them ([In Compliance](https://incompliancemag.com/lightning-surge-damage-to-ethernet-and-pots-ports-connected-to-inside-wiring/); [Sandman](https://www.sandman.com/GroundingBull.html)).

| Step | Action | What "good" looks like |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | At the NID's customer side, unplug the test-jack plug (or cut and cap the incoming pair on an old block). | Inside wiring is now physically separate from the outside plant. |
| 2 | Measure DC across red/green (line 1) and black/yellow (line 2) at a jack. | **0 V**. A reading near 48 V means a live or dry telco loop is still attached; stop and fix step 1. |
| 3 | With everything unplugged, check continuity between all pair combinations; never use the ohmmeter with the NID plug still connected ([Natural Handyman](https://www.naturalhandyman.com/iip/inftelephone/inftel4.html)). | No shorts between pairs. Don't trust colors: green-with-black miswires are common ([Tractor By Net](https://www.tractorbynet.com/forums/threads/help-with-phone-problem.28118/post-308016)). |
| 4 | Plug the HT801's green FXS port into a basement jack (or wire it to the NID customer-side line-1 terminals). Connect the blue port to Ethernet, then apply power. | ATA boots; lights indicate network. |
| 5 | Upstairs, measure the line-1 pair on-hook, then lift a phone and listen. | About -48 V on-hook, a few volts off-hook; dial tone present. |
| 6 | Find the ATA's IP by dialing `***`, then `02`. Log in to the web UI (default admin/admin) and change the password. | Web UI loads (it is slow; refresh patiently). |
| 7 | On the FXS Port tab, set **Offhook Auto-Dial** to HA's IP with `*` for dots plus the port, for example `*47192*168*1*100*5060`. Set **Preferred Vocoder** to OPUS (payload 123) and make it the only one. | Lifting the handset now calls HA directly ([HA tutorial](https://www.home-assistant.io/voice_control/worlds-most-private-voice-assistant/)). |
| 8 | Give HA a fixed IP or DHCP reservation, so the auto-dial string never breaks. Do the same for the ATA. | Stable addressing. |
| 9 | In HA OS, install Speech-to-Phrase, Piper and (optionally) Whisper. Confirm the Wyoming integration discovers them, then build an Assist pipeline with the built-in agent ([HA local guide](https://www.home-assistant.io/voice_control/voice_remote_local_assistant/)). | Pipeline testable from the HA UI. |
| 10 | Add the **VoIP** integration and pick up the phone; you hear a setup message. On the new device, enable **Allow calls**, select your local pipeline, and set finished-speaking detection (start with Relaxed). | The next pickup plays a listening tone and executes commands ([SmartHomeScene](https://smarthomescene.com/guides/voice-calling-home-assistant-with-an-old-phone)). |
| 11 | Test an outbound `assist_satellite.announce` to the phone. | Phone rings; TTS loops until you hang up. |

If a call connects but nothing happens, open the ATA's Status page. Hook state should flip from "On Hook" to "In Use" when you lift the handset; if it doesn't, reboot the ATA ([HA tutorial](https://www.home-assistant.io/voice_control/worlds-most-private-voice-assistant/)). A failed test call almost always means the Opus vocoder or payload type is wrong.

## Five ways to grow from one phone to several

The expansion decision turns on one question: do the phones need to be **separate assistants**, or are they just **more places to pick up the same one**? Phones wired in parallel on one FXS port all ring together, share one line, and look to the ATA and to HA like a single loop. There is no way to know which handset was lifted ([HA VoIP docs](https://www.home-assistant.io/integrations/voip/)). HA keys devices on the caller's SIP URI, so per-phone identity, per-room pipelines and per-room announcements all require one ATA identity per phone ([devices.py](https://github.com/home-assistant/core/blob/dev/homeassistant/components/voip/devices.py)). Multi-port ATAs do not get around this natively. HA's docs still say only one port works on an HT802 or HT812, and the 2023 feature request for multiple lines was never implemented ([HA VoIP docs](https://www.home-assistant.io/integrations/voip/); [HA forum](https://community.home-assistant.io/t/support-multiple-lines-in-voip-integration/568949)).

| Path | How | Phones / identity | Cost | Best when |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A. Parallel on line 1 | Just plug more phones into existing jacks on the backfed pair | Up to **5 REN** total (old bell ringers ~1 REN each; electronic phones usually less); **one shared HA device** | $0 | You want "pick up anywhere, talk to the house" |
| B. Second HT801 on line 2 | Backfeed a second HT801 into the black/yellow pair; re-terminate chosen jacks to line 2 (black to line-2 green, yellow to line-2 red) ([homephonewiring.com](https://homephonewiring.com/connect2.html)) | Two independent HA devices, each with its own pipeline and announcements | ~$41–53 | Two zones (e.g., kitchen vs. bedroom) on typical two-pair wiring |
| C. One HT801 per home run | If jacks are star-wired back to the basement, give each run its own HT801 | N independent devices, natively supported | ~$45 per phone | Each phone must be its own room assistant, with no PBX |
| D. PBX layer (FreePBX/Asterisk or HACS VoIP Stack) with an HT812/HT814/HT818 | ATA ports register to the PBX; Assist becomes a dialable extension; the PBX transcodes to Opus | Many extensions; intercom, ring groups, DTMF menus | HT812 ~$65, HT814 ~$73–88, plus PBX effort | You want phones to call each other, keypad codes, or central management |
| E. DECT base on an FXS port | Plug an analog cordless base into a jack | All handsets share that line | Varies | Coverage where no jack exists |

Paths A through C keep the elegant direct-dial design and the officially supported code path. Several HT801s are also the cheapest route to per-phone identity, because the multi-port units buy you nothing natively. Path D is the only route to **keypad input**. HA's VoIP integration advertises telephone-event in its SDP but silently drops non-Opus RTP packets, DTMF included, and exposes no DTMF entities or events ([voip-utils voip.py](https://github.com/home-assistant-libs/voip-utils/blob/master/voip_utils/voip.py)). With FreePBX, users report that a Grandstream HT818v2's extensions each appear as separate HA endpoints assignable to rooms ([HA forum](https://community.home-assistant.io/t/voice-over-ip-integration-call-from-any-sip-softphone/567762?page=5)). The PBX path has its own costs, though. You must restrict the trunk to **Opus only**, because the "unable to find a codec translation path (ulaw) to (opus)" failure is common. One guide author reports that all phones appeared from the PBX IP, and that Assist failed to hang up, leaving the next caller a busy signal ([HA forum](https://community.home-assistant.io/t/voice-over-ip-integration-call-from-any-sip-softphone/567762?page=5); [FreePBX forum](https://community.freepbx.org/t/installing-and-configuring-voice-over-ip-for-home-assistant-connecting-to-freepbx/107798)). VoIP Stack is the lighter alternative. It supports RFC 4733/SIP INFO DTMF, G.711, L16 and Opus, intercom and IVR via automations, and requires HA 2026.7 or later. But it is a young single-maintainer project with no SRTP, and nobody has documented it with Grandstream FXS ports and touch-tone menus ([HA forum](https://community.home-assistant.io/t/voip-stack-for-home-assistant-and-esphome/1016178)).

Mind the ringing load as you add phones in parallel. Per-port REN is **5 on the HT801, 3 on the HT812, and 2 on the HT802/HT814/HT818** ([Grandstream FAQ](https://documentation.grandstream.com/knowledge-base/ht8xx-v1-faq/); [HT81x V2 guide](https://documentation.grandstream.com/knowledge-base/ht81x-v2-user-guide/)). Multi-port ATAs suit one phone per port, while the HT801 is the one to use for a whole-house parallel pair.

## Pitfalls that bite phone builds specifically

Some failures recur so often in forum and GitHub reports that you should plan for them up front. **Codec**: anything but Opus/123 fails. **New devices are blocked** until you flip "Allow calls" ([HA tutorial](https://www.home-assistant.io/voice_control/worlds-most-private-voice-assistant/)). **Port 5060** collides with any other SIP software on the host; move the integration's SIP port if needed ([HA VoIP docs](https://www.home-assistant.io/integrations/voip/)). **Hanging up too soon** can make HA call you back ([HA forum](https://community.home-assistant.io/t/how-to-make-grandstream-ht802-ring-via-home-assistant/766987)). Timers set by voice are delivered the same way, by HA ringing the phone ([assist_satellite.py](https://github.com/home-assistant/core/blob/dev/homeassistant/components/voip/assist_satellite.py)). **Announcements loop until someone hangs up**, which suits doorbells and alarms but grates for routine information. Back-to-back announcements can swallow the second, and only TTS is supported, not media files ([dev.to](https://dev.to/clarkbw--/no-audio-from-the-home-assistant-nabu-puck-use-assistsatelliteannounce-li3); [strings.json](https://github.com/home-assistant/core/blob/dev/homeassistant/components/voip/strings.json)). Outbound calls dial the device's stored SIP contact, so call HA from each new phone once before you rely on announcements.

Reliability history argues for staying on current releases and keeping a recovery automation ready. A 2024.10 refactor broke HT801 setups for some users for months ([community thread](https://community.home-assistant.io/t/2024-10-breaks-voip-integration/781895)). A 2026 "dead air" bug left calls connecting with no pipeline after a caller hung up mid-response, and only a Core restart cleared it. The causes included a stale pipeline task and an RTP sequence-number overflow, and the issue was still open in October 2026. The current dev code carries the apparent fixes (a 2.0 s hang-up timeout and voip-utils 0.5.1) ([GitHub #175718](https://github.com/home-assistant/core/issues/175718); [manifest.json](https://github.com/home-assistant/core/blob/dev/homeassistant/components/voip/manifest.json)). An automation that reloads the VoIP config entry is a cheaper stopgap than restarting Core. On the analog side, consumer FXS ports ring below the roughly 90 Vrms of a central office, and Grandstream publishes no ring-voltage figure. **Vintage electromechanical bell phones may ring weakly**, so prefer electronic-ringer touch-tone sets ([Cisco smb2780](https://www.cisco.com/c/en/us/support/docs/smb/unified-communications/cisco-small-business-voice-gateways-and-atas/smb2780-troubleshoot-a-spa112-and-a-spa122-when-the-connected-phones.html)). HA has no VoIP gain controls. Tune levels with the ATA's FXS TX/RX gain, keep its line echo cancellation on, and use the Relaxed VAD setting if commands get clipped ([Grandstream HT80x guide](https://documentation.grandstream.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/HT80x_Admin_Guide.pdf)). Finally, avoid end-of-life ATAs: Obihai support ended in December 2023 and Cisco's SPA112 is end-of-life ([OBiTALK](https://mobile.obitalk.com/info/faq/About-the-OBi/end-of-life-announcement); [Sangoma KB](https://sangomakb.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/SND/pages/403669091/How+do+I+get+my+analog+phones+that+are+attached+to+a+Cisco+SPA112+or+SPA122+to+ring)).

### What remains unverified

Several questions that matter to your build had no source in the research. No one has published an HA-specific write-up of backfeeding an ATA into in-wall wiring, although generic whole-house ATA backfeeding is standard. No source shows how cleanly two parallel phones off-hook at once share audio on a Grandstream port, or gives the actual loop current and ring voltage of the HT80x. The NID disconnect method varies by model, and NEC Article 800 was not reviewed. FCC Part 68 likely does not apply with no public-network connection, but that is an inference. It is unconfirmed whether the HT802's second port works through a PBX with a distinct HA identity, which HA release first shipped the dead-air fixes, and which release made `start_conversation` work with the non-LLM agent. No one has measured Speech-to-Phrase accuracy or end-to-end latency on HT801/Opus phone audio. VoIP Stack's behavior with FXS ports and touch-tone menus is untested. Treat each of these as something to verify on your own bench before committing to a multi-phone design.

## Conclusion

Your empty house wiring is an underrated asset. Every other phone-assistant build fights to get audio and power to the phone, while yours inherits a pre-run, star- or daisy-chained distribution network that only needs one $45 box at the demarc to come alive. The sharper insight concerns scaling. HA's integration identifies callers by ATA, not by port or handset, so the wiring topology you discover with a meter in step 3 effectively decides your expansion ceiling. A daisy chain gives you at most two independent assistants (line 1 and line 2), while home runs give you one per jack. Map your pairs before you buy a second ATA.

The software choice inverts the usual voice-assistant wisdom. On a phone, the open-ended, LLM-heavy stack performs worst: Whisper struggles with telephone-band audio and the VoIP path cannot stream TTS. Meanwhile the humble closed-vocabulary Speech-to-Phrase plus built-in intents is both the most private and the most responsive option. Start there, add an LLM on separate hardware only for genuine Q&A, and move to a PBX only when you actually want phones to call each other or the keypad to do something. That threshold is where the system stops being a supported appliance and becomes a telephony project.
