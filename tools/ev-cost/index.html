Where the defaults come from, and what they leave out

Gas price

  • Statistics Canada Table 18-10-0001-01, monthly regular self-serve price, average of Calgary and Edmonton (data to September 2026). Annual averages: 2021 126¢, 2022 155¢, 2023 136¢, 2024 145¢, 2025 132¢. The default is the 5-year average (Oct 2021 – Sep 2026), 143.4¢/L.
  • Caveat: April 2021 – March 2025 prices include the federal consumer carbon levy (up to 17.6¢/L), which has since been removed, while 2022–23 prices were lowered by Alberta's fuel-tax relief. Historical averages are a starting point, not a forecast. 2026 has run well above them.

Electricity

  • Energy charge: Alberta's Rate of Last Resort is fixed at about 12.0¢/kWh for 2025–2026 (EPCOR, ENMAX, ATCO and FortisAlberta territories, via the Utilities Consumer Advocate). Before 2025 the old Regulated Rate swung widely, from about 10¢ to over 30¢ in 2022–23. Fixed-rate retailer contracts can be lower.
  • Delivery and fees: ENMAX's 2026 per-kWh transmission and distribution (about 5.45¢) plus Calgary's local access fee (about 1.55¢). Rural FortisAlberta customers and other cities differ, so check a recent bill. Only per-kWh charges are counted, since the daily fixed fees don't change when you add an EV. GST (5%) is added.
  • Charging losses (about 10%) and the cold-weather allowance are applied on top of the car's rated efficiency.

Insurance, tax and rebate

  • Insurance: Surex's 2025 analysis of 12 months of quotes put Alberta averages at $2,344/yr for gas cars and $3,343/yr for EVs, the widest gap in Canada. These are averages across different vehicles, not like-for-like, so a real quote for your two cars is the single most valuable number to plug in here.
  • Alberta charges a $200 annual tax on battery-electric vehicles at registration, in addition to normal registration (plug-in hybrids are exempt). Normal registration costs the same for both, so it's left out.
  • Federal Electric Vehicle Affordability Program (from February 2026): up to $5,000 off a new battery-electric vehicle at the dealer, with a $50,000 transaction cap unless the car is built in Canada, eligible countries of manufacture only, and one rebate per person. The amounts are scheduled to decline in later years, so confirm the current figure with the dealer.

Maintenance and resale

  • CAA estimates EV owners save 40–50% on maintenance (no oil changes, less brake wear). Defaults of $1,000 versus $550 a year reflect that. EVs can wear tires faster, which this range already allows for.
  • Depreciation rates are estimates: roughly a third of the price remains after 8 years for a gas car and a quarter for an EV. Used-EV prices fell sharply in 2023–24, which makes this the most uncertain default here.
  • Resale value is worked out from the price after the rebate, on the basis that used prices follow what a car effectively costs new.

Not modelled

  • Battery replacement. Most EV batteries carry an 8-year / 160,000 km warranty, so it rarely matters within a typical ownership period.
  • Plug-in hybrids, time-of-use electricity plans, free workplace charging, the value of your time at fast chargers, and emissions.
  • Winter tires, parking and normal registration, which cost about the same for both.
