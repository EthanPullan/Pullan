Should I switch to an EV?

Compare four choices in Alberta: keep the car you have, buy a new gas car, buy a new EV, or buy a used EV. It counts the purchase, rebate, financing, fuel versus charging, insurance, maintenance, the provincial EV tax, resale value, and the investment growth you give up on money spent on the car. Most sliders start on a researched Alberta figure (sources at the bottom). Set the "current car" and "used EV" sliders to the cars you're looking at.

Total cost if you sell at the end of each year

Everything you've paid so far plus the investment growth it cost you, minus what the car would sell for. Where lines cross, the cheaper choice changes.

Where the money goes

Every option starts from the car you own today. If you keep it, its current value counts as a cost because you could have sold it. If you buy, it's sold to help pay, so that value nets out.

Year-by-year numbers

Where the defaults come from, and what they leave out

Gas price

  • Statistics Canada Table 18-10-0001-01, monthly regular self-serve price, average of Calgary and Edmonton (data to September 2026). Annual averages: 2021 126¢, 2022 155¢, 2023 136¢, 2024 145¢, 2025 132¢. The default is the 5-year average (Oct 2021 – Sep 2026), 143.4¢/L.
  • Caveat: April 2021 – March 2025 prices include the federal consumer carbon levy (up to 17.6¢/L), which has since been removed, while 2022–23 prices were lowered by Alberta's fuel-tax relief. Historical averages are a starting point, not a forecast. 2026 has run well above them.

Electricity

  • Energy charge: the default is 8.29¢/kWh, in line with 2-year fixed-rate contracts offered in 2026 (for example SPOT Power 8.29¢, Encor 8.25¢, Direct Energy 8.37¢; some come with a daily admin fee or a deposit). If you haven't signed a contract you're on the Rate of Last Resort, fixed at about 12.0¢/kWh for 2025–2026. Before 2025 the old Regulated Rate swung widely, from about 10¢ to over 30¢ in 2022–23.
  • Delivery and fees: ENMAX's 2026 per-kWh transmission and distribution (about 5.45¢) plus Calgary's local access fee (about 1.55¢). Rural FortisAlberta customers and other cities differ, so check a recent bill. Only per-kWh charges are counted, since the daily fixed fees don't change when you add an EV. GST (5%) is added.
  • Charging losses (about 10%) and the cold-weather allowance are applied on top of the car's rated efficiency.
  • Public charging: Paren's network data puts Alberta's average DC fast-charging price at $0.67/kWh in Q2 2026 (down from $0.72 a year earlier, against about $0.50 nationally; free chargers are excluded). Many Alberta chargers bill by the minute or hour instead, for example Edmonton's Encor network at $18/hour for fast charging and $2/hour for Level 2. Public Level 2 generally costs $1–$2.50 an hour, and some municipal sites are free.

Insurance, tax and rebate

  • Insurance: Surex's 2025 analysis of 12 months of quotes put Alberta averages at $2,344/yr for gas cars and $3,343/yr for EVs, the widest gap in Canada. These are averages across different vehicles, not like-for-like, so a real quote for your two cars is the single most valuable number to plug in here.
  • Premiums falling with value: about half of a typical Alberta premium is collision and comprehensive cover (MyChoice's 2026 median quote: $944 of $1,952), and that's the part that depends on what the car is worth. Repair costs don't fall as a car ages, so the default assumes 40% of each premium falls in step with the car's value, on top of the yearly increase. The insurance figure you enter is for year 1. If you'd drop collision cover on an older car, lower its insurance figure instead.
  • Alberta charges a $200 annual tax on battery-electric vehicles at registration, in addition to normal registration (plug-in hybrids are exempt). Normal registration costs the same for both, so it's left out.
  • Federal Electric Vehicle Affordability Program (from February 2026): up to $5,000 off a new battery-electric vehicle at the dealer, falling to $4,000 on January 1, 2027. There's a $50,000 transaction cap unless the car is built in Canada, only eligible countries of manufacture qualify, and it's one rebate per person.
  • Used EVs get no rebate in Alberta: the federal program only covers vehicles registered for the first time, and Alberta has no provincial EV rebate, new or used. (Some other provinces, such as Quebec, do offer used-EV rebates.)

Maintenance and resale

  • CAA estimates EV owners save 40–50% on maintenance (no oil changes, less brake wear). Defaults of $1,000 versus $550 a year reflect that. EVs can wear tires faster, which this range already allows for.
  • Depreciation rates are estimates: roughly a third of the price remains after 8 years for a gas car and a quarter for an EV. Used-EV prices fell sharply in 2023–24, which makes this the most uncertain default here.
  • All car prices are entered before tax and 5% GST is added (new cars always, a used EV only from a dealer). Resale value is worked out from the pre-tax price, since GST isn't part of what a car is worth, and for a new EV after the rebate, on the basis that used prices follow what a car effectively costs new.
  • Rebate and GST: the dealer is understood to apply GST to the full price first and then take the rebate off, so the rebate doesn't reduce the GST. That's per most guides citing Transport Canada's dealer guidance; one guide says the opposite, so confirm with the dealer.

Used EVs

  • GST: Alberta has no PST. A dealer charges 5% GST on a used car, and a private sale of a used vehicle has none. The "Buying from" toggle adds GST to the asking price for dealer sales only. Resale is based on the pre-tax asking price.
  • The used EV uses the same driving, charging, electricity and home-charger settings as the new EV, so it assumes a similar model. It pays the $200 Alberta EV tax as well.
  • Battery: most EVs from 2020 on carry an 8-year / 160,000 km battery warranty that transfers to later owners. The calculator works out how many warranty years are left from the car's age, then adds your battery allowance for each year after that. Check the odometer too, because high-mileage cars can reach the km limit first. A third-party battery health report costs about $100–$150, and recent data across 30 brands puts the average battery health around 93%.
  • Used-EV prices were volatile in 2026: a record drop in March, then a rebound as inventory thinned and gas prices rose. The used-EV insurance, maintenance and depreciation defaults are estimates, not Alberta data.

Your current car and the cost of money

  • All three options start from the car you own today. If you buy, it's sold or traded in the day you buy. If you keep it, the money you could have had by selling it counts as a cost, and the car keeps losing value and needing more repairs each year. The differences between options come out the same either way. This just keeps the totals readable.
  • The "current car" defaults (value, insurance, repairs) are placeholders, not Alberta data. Set them to your own car and your own insurance bill.
  • Investment growth given up: every dollar that leaves your pocket (down payment, loan payments, fuel, insurance, repairs) is treated as money that would otherwise have earned your expected return, and money coming back (selling your current car, resale at the end) as money that starts earning it. The totals are how much smaller your investments would be at the end. This treats cash buyers and borrowers the same way: a loan cheaper than your expected return comes out ahead of paying cash. Set the return to 0% to ignore it.
  • 5% is a rough long-run figure for a balanced portfolio held in a TFSA, before inflation. If the money would otherwise pay down a mortgage or other debt, use that interest rate instead.

Not modelled

  • Battery replacement. Most EV batteries carry an 8-year / 160,000 km warranty, so it rarely matters within a typical ownership period.
  • Plug-in hybrids, time-of-use electricity plans, free workplace charging, the value of your time at fast chargers, and emissions.
  • Winter tires, parking and normal registration, which cost about the same for both.
